## Interpreting User Needs

I think interpreting user needs starts with understanding what the user wants the program to accomplish rather than immediately focusing on how to develop it. User stories help with this by breaking larger requirements into smaller, more manageable features. They also help developers understand the purpose behind each feature. During the SNHU Travel project, user stories helped organize features such as destination recommendations, price limits, and special deals. Having clear acceptance criteria also makes it easier to determine whether those features are working as intended.

## My Approach to Software Development

My approach to developing programs is to establish the main objectives first and then break the work into smaller tasks. I like a clear objective while still having room for changes or improvements throughout development. One of the biggest things I learned from Scrum is how useful that balance can be. In future projects, I would seek to incorporate different practices such as Sprint Planning, regular communication, testing, etc. I think these practices can help maintain progress without requiring every detail to be finalized from the beginning.

## Being a Good Team Member

I believe being a good team member in software development comes down to communication, accountability, and cooperation. Everyone should understand their purposese, communicate problems when they crop up, and be willing to help one another when needed. During my Scrum Master assignments, I focused on leading by example and making sure the team could maintain progress without  confusion. I also think being open to feedback is important because software development often involves making adjustments as a project progresses.
